A sports or modified car doesn't sell like a family sedan. There are fewer buyers, more tire-kickers, and almost nobody values the work you put into it. These are your two options and how to choose.

## Option 1: sell it yourself

- **Pro:** you don't pay a commission.
- **Con:** you answer dozens of messages, deal with no-shows and get offers far below what it's worth.
- **Risk:** test drives with strangers and payments that aren't always what they seem.

## Option 2: sell it on consignment

An intermediary presents the car, screens buyers and accompanies the test drives. You only approve the final price.

- **Pro:** the car reaches people who are really looking for that kind of car, and you don't waste time.
- **What you should demand:** a written contract with the minimum price, the commission, the duration and who pays each expense.
- **Golden rule:** they only get paid if it sells.

## How to set the price

1. Look for listings of the same model and year with similar mileage.
2. Take the average price and subtract 5% to 10%: that's what deals usually actually close at.
3. Modifications **are almost never recovered 100%**. Value the car as if it were stock and add only what a buyer would pay for quality parts with an invoice.

> If you kept the original parts, say so in the listing: for many buyers a car that can go back to factory spec is worth more.

## Before listing

- Have the maintenance and modification invoices at hand.
- Make sure the modifications are legal for the inspection (exhaust, ride height, window tint, lights).
- Wash the car thoroughly and take photos in daylight, flaws included.
