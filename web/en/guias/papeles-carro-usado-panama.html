Paperwork for buying a used car in Panama

Which documents to check before paying, how to avoid other people's fines and liens, and how to close the title transfer safely.

The car can be perfect and still cause you problems if the paperwork isn't in order: fines you inherit, a bank lien or a title transfer that never gets completed. This is the list we use before paying a single dollar.

Before seeing the car

  • Ask whether the seller is the registered owner and can show you the vehicle registration.
  • Ask for the chassis number (VIN) to check the history if it's imported.
  • Ask whether the inspection (revisado) and clearance certificate (paz y salvo) are up to date, and whether the car is financed.

When you have the papers in hand

  1. The vehicle registration is in the seller's name.
  2. The seller's ID (cédula) matches the registration.
  3. The VIN on the chassis, on the windshield and on the papers are the same.
  4. The engine number matches the registration.
  5. Check with the ATTT that it has no fines or restrictions.
  6. Verify that the municipal clearance certificate and the inspection are current.
  7. Confirm it has no liens or encumbrances. If it's financed, ask for the bank's letter with the balance and how it will be paid off.

If something doesn't add up, stop. There are plenty of cars; a legal problem can cost you the whole car.

Closing day

  • Sign a purchase agreement with both parties' details, the VIN, the engine number, the mileage and the price. Add an annex with the known flaws.
  • Pay by bank transfer at the moment you sign the title transfer. Never pay in advance without a signature.
  • Get all the keys, the manual and the maintenance invoices.
  • Start the title transfer as soon as possible and keep a copy of everything.

Requirements can change: always confirm the current procedure with the ATTT and your municipality.
